# Scene Drift

## The Simple Definition

Scene Drift is the GM's **Else statement**.

It answers one question:

**What changes if the players do nothing?**

Do not treat Drift as atmosphere, foreshadowing, or a hidden plot branch. It is the pressure that advances when the characters hesitate, wait, argue, fail to act, or fail to interrupt the active threat in the scene.

Scene Drift lives inside the **Scene Card**. It is read beside Ground, Will, and Shift:

- **Ground:** What currently holds.
- **Will:** What is already pushing.
- **Shift:** What changes when characters act.
- **Drift:** What worsens if unresolved pressure meets a stated trigger.

This makes Drift part of scene management, not a separate pacing note.

---

## The Core Principle

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Drift is Trigger-Based and Event-Driven.**
>
> Drift advances when **unresolved pressure has reached a stated trigger** on the active Scene Card. It does not advance automatically at the end of every round unless the Scene Card defines end-of-round escalation as one of its triggers (e.g., when timing itself is the active pressure, such as rising floodwaters, poison gas, or alarm cycles).

---

## The Tabletop: Maps vs. Scene Cards

Scene Cards augment maps, VTTs, props, miniatures, tokens, handouts, stat blocks, and ordinary table procedures. They do not replace them.

* **Maps show space.**
* **Scene Cards show pressure.**

Terminus remains fully compatible with traditional tabletop and VTT play. While a map or physical setup shows the physical geometry of a room (rubble, channels, doors), the Scene Card acts as the "state overlay," telling the Guide what those physical elements currently *mean in active play*.

## The Executable Rule

Drift must be written as an executable state. The Guide should not have to interpret when Drift happens or what it means. It triggers on concrete events, changes the scene, and gives the players a new state to respond to.

### Bad Drift (Vague or Default-Timer):
- The ritual will eventually succeed. (Too vague)
- Tension rises. (Purely narrative, no mechanism)
- Drift increases automatically at the end of every round. (Should not be a universal default; some scenes may still use end-of-round Drift when timing itself is the active pressure, but it must be explicitly defined by the card)
- The enemy prepares something worse. (Not executable)

### Good Drift (Trigger and State-Aware):
- **When the party hesitates in the open or delays crossing:** +1 Drift.
- **When blood hits the ancient stones or an attack fails critically:** +1 Drift.
- **When the alarm bell rings or a signal is ignored:** +1 Drift.

When Drift causes threshold loss, name the kind of burden:
- **Endure:** The body absorbs physical wear, exposure, impact, smoke, heat, pressure, or injury.
- **Avoid:** The character loses safe position, footing, cover, timing, or tactical angle.
- **Exert:** The character loses inner reserve, control, concentration, courage, or magical stability.

## Hazard vs. Trap

Drift also classifies the threat type.

### Hazard
A scene is a Hazard when Drift means the environment deteriorates and waiting makes survival harder.

Use Hazard Drift for:
- fire, smoke, floodwater
- collapse, contamination, exposure
- unstable terrain, failing civic infrastructure

*Examples:*
- **Trigger:** When characters delay or stand in the flood channel: +1 Drift. (At Drift 2, water reaches knees, risking Avoid loss; at Drift 3, low exits are submerged).
- **Trigger:** When characters trigger a mechanical fracture or a Working fails critically: +1 Drift. (At Drift 2, hot ash fills the corridor, dealing 1 Endure collateral Impact to unprotected targets).

### Trap
A scene is a Trap when Drift escalates an active intent, response, or mechanism.

Use Trap Drift for:
- alerts, pursuit, lockouts
- reinforcements, targeting
- reset cycles, adaptive mechanisms

*Examples:*
- **Trigger:** When a guard spots the party, or if they debate loud plans in the passage: +1 Drift. (At Drift 2, one squad enters alert posture; at Drift 3, the sector locks down).
- **Trigger:** When a character fails to bypass a pressure lock or ignores a terminal alarm: +1 Drift. (At Drift 2, the lock arms defensive plates; at Drift 3, the chamber purges).

---

## Writing Rules

1. Define concrete **Trigger Events** (hesitating under pressure, letting an alarm sound, failing a key check).
2. Name a visible state change (water rising, gates descending, signals flashing).
3. Make the change alter player options immediately.
4. Avoid vague pacing words like "eventually," "soon," or "tension rises."
5. If Drift does not change what the players can safely do next, rewrite it.

---

## Scene Card Formula

Use this compressed form:

**Drift:** When [Trigger Event occurs], add 1 Drift.
* **Drift 1:** [Immediate narrative or minor effect]
* **Drift 2:** [Environmental hazard/effect altering player options]
* **Drift 3:** [Systemic lock/rupture forcing immediate response or rout]
