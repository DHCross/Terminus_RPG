# Scene Drift

## The Simple Definition

Scene Drift is the GM's **Else statement**.

It answers one question:

**What changes if the players do nothing?**

Do not treat Drift as atmosphere, foreshadowing, or a hidden plot branch. It is the pressure that advances while the characters hesitate, wait, argue, search, or fail to interrupt the scene.

Scene Drift lives inside the **Scene Card**. It is read beside Ground, Will, and Shift:

- **Ground:** what currently holds.
- **Will:** what is already pushing.
- **Shift:** what changes when characters act.
- **Drift:** what changes if they do nothing.

This makes Drift part of scene management, not a separate pacing note.

## The Executable Rule

Drift must be written as an executable state.

Bad Drift:

- The ritual will eventually succeed.
- The room grows more dangerous.
- Tension rises.
- The enemy prepares something worse.

Good Drift:

- At the end of each round, the water rises 5 feet.
- At the end of each round, one more summoned entity enters the circle.
- At the end of each round, the south exit locks.
- At the end of each round, all characters in smoke lose 1 Endure circle unless protected.

The GM should not have to interpret when Drift happens or what it means. It triggers, changes the scene, and gives the players a new state to respond to.

When Drift causes threshold loss, name the kind of burden:

- **Endure:** the body absorbs physical wear, exposure, impact, smoke, heat, pressure, or injury.
- **Avoid:** the character loses safe position, footing, cover, timing, or tactical angle.
- **Exert:** the character loses inner reserve, control, concentration, courage, or magical stability.

## The Rhythm Engine

Drift is Terminus's rhythm engine.

It removes the GM burden of inventing complications to keep scenes moving. If the players stall, Drift advances. If they act, Shift answers them. If they do nothing, Drift answers them.

Drift is not fake urgency. It is the future asserting itself.

## Hazard vs Trap

Drift also classifies the threat.

### Hazard

A scene is a Hazard when Drift means the environment deteriorates and waiting makes survival harder.

Use Hazard Drift for:

- fire
- smoke
- floodwater
- collapse
- contamination
- exposure
- pressure
- unstable terrain
- failing civic infrastructure

Examples:

- At the end of each round, the water rises 5 feet.
- At the end of each round, one more floor section becomes unsafe.
- At the end of each round, unprotected characters lose 1 Endure circle to smoke.

### Trap

A scene is a Trap when Drift escalates an active intent, response, or mechanism.

Use Trap Drift for:

- alerts
- pursuit
- lockouts
- reinforcements
- targeting
- reset cycles
- adaptive mechanisms
- enemies becoming better positioned

Examples:

- At the end of each round, one guard squad enters alert posture.
- At the end of each round, the vault locks one additional exit.
- At the end of each round, the mechanism selects a new target.

## Writing Rules

1. Start with "At the end of each round..."
2. Name a visible state change.
3. Make the change alter player options.
4. Avoid vague pacing words like "eventually," "soon," or "tension rises."
5. If Drift does not change what the players can safely do next, rewrite it.

## Scene Card Formula

Use this compressed form:

**Drift:** At the end of each round, [specific state change]. If this reaches [threshold], [new scene state].

Examples:

- **Hazard:** At the end of each round, the water rises 5 feet. At 15 feet, the lower exits are submerged.
- **Trap:** At the end of each round, one guard squad enters alert posture. At 3 squads, the courtyard locks down.
- **Rupture:** At the end of each round, one civic sign displays yesterday's instruction. At 4 signs, the street repeats the last ten minutes.
