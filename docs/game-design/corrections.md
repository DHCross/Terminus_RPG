# Corrections

## Backstage / Guide Truth

**Corrections**
The hidden function by which the buried order attempts to repair, erase, isolate, or reassign incoherent regions of Tringad.

Corrections are not monsters in the conventional sense. They are not creatures that hunger, migrate, or reproduce. They are responses — enforcement manifestations produced by an architecture that predates living memory and does not require permission to act.

The buried order does not announce corrections. It performs them.

Do not call them Terminus Agents in-world. That framing is too revealing of architecture the players should not name until much later.

---

## Accord-Facing Classification

These are the terms the Sixfold Accord uses in official field reports, incident logs, and Correction Writs. The vocabulary is dry, administrative, and frightening precisely because of its restraint. Accord language does not editorialize. It categorizes.

**Correction Body**
A manifested enforcement form. Humanoid or near-humanoid in structure. May carry, display, or embody sealed orders. The Accord does not speculate about its origin. It classifies the shape and logs the behavior.

**Correction Instrument**
A tool-like manifestation. Not necessarily humanoid. May be architectural, mechanical, or procedural in behavior. Correction Instruments are classified when a manifested form appears to be performing a function rather than enacting a will.

**Correction Writ**
A nonphysical or semi-legal manifestation: impossible notices, unreceived summons, documents with no sender, seals that have already been affixed, redactions that have already occurred. A Correction Writ may precede or follow a Correction Body. It may also appear alone.

**Correction Office**
A place that has begun enforcing its own reality. A Correction Office does not move. It expands. Rooms develop new purpose. Documents appear that were not filed. Doors open to spaces that should not exist. The Accord classifies a location as a Correction Office when the enforcement is architectural rather than embodied. While players can physically fight a Correction Body (a mechanical entity), they must take a completely different approach to a Correction Office. An Office occurs when a room or environment begins enforcing an automated law that no one can survive, forcing the players to interpret, bypass, appeal, or nullify the active permissions rather than just swinging a weapon.

**Unauthorized Correction**
Accord term for an entity acting without a known civic source. This classification is an acknowledgment of ignorance, not a description of behavior. The Accord uses it when a Correction Body cannot be traced to any known writ, institution, or licensure — which is, in practice, always.

## Common Folk Names

These names are local, superstitious, and uneven. They are not coined by scholars. They are used by dockworkers, innkeepers, travelers who came back wrong, and travelers who did not come back. Expect regional variation. Expect contradiction.

**The Black Walker**
Human and Pinder name for a humanoid Correction Body. The color association varies: sometimes it is the clothing, sometimes the shadow it casts, sometimes the absence of color in the space around it. The name is older than any living speaker.

**The Bell-Giant**
For towering Correction Bodies that produce chimes, bells, or rhythmic signal behavior. In some port districts, the Bell-Giant is understood as a labor warning — something that tolls when a working has gone wrong. No one stays to hear the full count.

**The Name-Taker**
For entities that erase, reassign, or invalidate identity. Not all of these are violent. Some Name-Takers simply stand near a person until that person's name is no longer recognized by civic systems, employment records, or family members.

**The Writ-Beast**
For Correction Bodies covered in hanging documents, seals, identification tags, and affixed notices. The Writ-Beast does not issue documents. It is covered in them. Whether those documents are its origin or its victims is not established.

**The Road Judge**
For Correction Bodies that appear on routes, crossings, bridges, and thoroughfares. The Road Judge does not block passage. It evaluates it. Most people who meet a Road Judge on the road do not stop to find out what the judgment was.

**The Door That Walks**
An excellent folk name for a sealed, rectangular, faceless Correction Body. Associated with endings, thresholds, and the wrong kind of transit. The Door That Walks is not understood as a creature. It is understood as an event.

## Stoneborn Names

Stoneborn naming for Correction Bodies draws from structural engineering, load assessment, and foundational judgment. The names are hard, load-bearing, and Norse-influenced. Stoneborn do not use monster names. They use failure-report names.

**Vardrek**
Translates roughly as "guard-drift." A Correction Body that appears when a structure — architectural, civic, or social — has been judged unsound. The Vardrek does not destroy the structure. It witnesses it until the structure can no longer hold.

**Kettil-Vard**
A containment form. Associated with pressure, sealed chambers, and things that are not permitted to expand further. Kettil-Vard appearances are logged by Stoneborn communities with the same seriousness as a building inspection that has already failed.

**Skeld Judge**
A plain translation-name that non-Stoneborn outsiders use when speaking to Stoneborn about Correction Bodies. Stoneborn find it imprecise but do not correct it. The term is close enough to be understood and too far away to matter.

**Load-Saint**
Folk name used by non-Stoneborn laborers who work alongside Stoneborn communities. The term is borrowed half-understood — the "saint" component is not Stoneborn theology, but laborers use it because something has to carry that weight.

## High Alfar Names

High Alfar naming is formal, memory-legal, and restrained. These are not monster names. They are the names of events in civic memory — things that have happened before and have been classified in the record. High Alfar understand Correction Bodies as old law moving without permission, which is disturbing not because of the violence but because of the precedent.

**The Witness Without Breath**
A Correction Body that observes without acting. The High Alfar classification implies that witnessing is the act — that something has been recorded, measured, or assessed without the assessed party's awareness or consent.

**The Accepted Shape**
A Correction Body that has been permitted to exist by some prior agreement that no living party remembers making. The High Alfar name is unsettling because it implies the Correction Body's presence is technically authorized — just not by anyone currently competent to revoke it.

**Seal-Walker**
A Correction Body associated with affixed authority: sealed orders, closed cases, finalized judgments. The Seal-Walker does not open seals. It is made of them, or is following them, or is what happens when a seal walks away from the document it was meant to close.

**Maerwyn's Refusal**
Named for a High Alfar archivist who declined to classify a specific Correction Body in a specific century. The refusal entered the record. The Correction Body's name is now the refusal itself. High Alfar find this appropriate. It means the entity is still being evaluated.

## Wild Alfar Names

Wild Alfar names for Correction Bodies use route language, refusal language, and the grammar of paths that no longer permit transit. To Wild Alfar, Correction Bodies are not entities — they are signs. Signs that a path has judged someone unfit to return.

**The Road That Refused**
Not a body — an event. A path that has stopped accepting a specific traveler. The traveler may not know which road this is until they find it. The Wild Alfar name implies no violence, only a door that will not open.

**The White Cut**
A Correction Body perceived as a line or boundary in the landscape that has become enforcement. The White Cut appears at the edge of a place that has been corrected — the boundary between what is still permitted and what has been reassigned.

**Cledwyn's Step**
Named for a Wild Alfar route-keeper who walked into a correction and did not return the same way. The name is used to describe the moment of encounter — the step you take that puts you into a Correction's jurisdiction. "Cledwyn's Step" is not the correction itself. It is the moment before.

**The Unreturning Thing**
For Correction Bodies or events that mark a transit as one-way. The Wild Alfar use this name for what others call monsters, roads, writs, or offices. The category is not about the entity's form. It is about what it means for travel.

---

## Deep Alfar Names

Deep Alfar naming is burial-oriented, record-oriented, and fundamentally concerned with what exists beneath. Deep Alfar Correction Body names are unsettling because they imply the Correction was already present before anyone arrived — waiting in the foundations, embedded in the records, filed under a name the building had not yet earned.

**The Below-Clerk**
A Correction Body that appears to have emerged from below the floor, the threshold, or the civic record. The Below-Clerk is not understood as having descended. It is understood as having already been there.

**The Foundation's Hand**
A Correction associated with structural judgment — not of buildings, but of the things built on agreements that have since failed. The Foundation's Hand does not demolish. It clarifies what the foundation was always deciding.

**Sithny's Mark**
Named for a Deep Alfar record-keeper whose marks — affixed to deeds, burial registers, and civic maps — continued to appear long after her recorded death. Sithny's Mark is used to describe any evidence that a Correction has already classified something before that thing existed.

**The Name Under Stone**
For Correction Bodies that have been found in civic records predating the civic institution they appear to serve. The Name Under Stone was filed before the building was built. It was waiting for the building to become what it became.

---

## Iseth / Archive Names

The Iseth are Egyptian-coded, record-state, and priestly in their relationship to Corrections. Their names for Correction Bodies are calm, taxonomic, and terrifying because they treat these events as administrative facts that require no extraordinary response — only accurate documentation.

**The Unclosed Case**
A Correction Body that represents a matter the Archive has not yet resolved. The Unclosed Case is not hostile. It is open. Its presence in a scene indicates that the Archive considers the scene's outcome unfinished.

**Khamat's Body**
Named for an Iseth archivist whose documented judgment — a finding against a particular district's coherence — was later found to have been the Correction itself. The body of the case became the body of enforcement. Iseth do not find this metaphysically strange. They find it procedurally significant.

**The Sun Ledger Walks**
For Correction Bodies associated with diurnal measurement, calendar enforcement, or the judgment of days that have been lived incorrectly. The Iseth name implies that the ledger has its own locomotion — not that it has been sent, but that it has determined the time has come to move.

**The Auditor of Noon**
For a Correction Body that arrives at the most legible moment — when everything is visible, accountable, and documented. The Auditor of Noon is not hidden. It does not appear in shadow. It appears when you can see it perfectly and have no grounds to dispute its presence.

## The Naming Rule

The broader principle from Terminus naming doctrine: names should reveal cultural expectation, not shared taxonomy.

A Pinder clerk encounters an unauthorized legal body.
A Stoneborn mason encounters a failed load calculation given shape.
A Wild Alfar traveler encounters a road refusing return.
A Deep Alfar librarian encounters the buried record walking upward.

The phenomenon is the same. The naming systems are not.

> *The Accord file calls it a Correction Body. The dockworkers call it a Bell-Giant. The Stoneborn mason refuses both names and says only, "The load has sent a witness."*

That is the tone.

## Guide Usage Notes

- Use **Corrections** as the official Guide-side department/function. This is the word you use in your notes, your Scene Cards, and your prep.
- Use **Correction Body / Correction Instrument / Correction Writ / Correction Office** as Accord field terminology in any document the players might encounter through the Sixfold Accord.
- Use local names in prose, NPC dialogue, and folk knowledge. Layer them by cultural context. Do not standardize.
- **Unauthorized Correction** is the Accord's category for what it cannot trace. Consider using this phrase when players request an official file on something the Accord has classified but cannot explain.
- A Correction Office scene is a location-based encounter structure, not a monster encounter. A Correction Office is not a monster in a room. It is a room whose Ground has become hostile law. Treat it as a Scene Card where the Ground itself is the threat: what is reliable, legal, and available in this space has been replaced by something that enforces a prior judgment. A Correction Body may also be present as the Will, but the Office does not require one.

  When running a Correction Office, the Scene Card question is not "What does the monster attack?" It is: **What does this room now refuse to permit?**

  A working Scene Card for a Correction Office:
  - **Ground:** The room has changed what is allowed. What was previously safe, legal, or traversable has been revised without announcement.
  - **Will:** The correction logic is actively trying to restore order. It does not negotiate. It enforces.
  - **Shift:** Player action triggers administrative response. Resistance, investigation, or movement invokes the correction's jurisdiction more fully.
  - **Drift:** Delay increases enforcement. Restriction, erasure, sealing, reassignment, and identity loss escalate the longer the scene runs without resolution.

- **Correction Bodies can be fought. Correction Offices have to be survived, interpreted, bypassed, appealed, corrupted, or made irrelevant.**

  This does not mean initiative is forbidden. If seconds matter, roll initiative. But initiative only orders action; it does not change the nature of the threat. In a Correction Office, the enemy is not something standing in the room. The enemy is what the room now refuses to permit.

  The design problem is not initiative. Initiative is a clock. The problem is genre reflex: the moment turn order begins, many Guides unconsciously shift into "kill the thing" mode. Terminus needs to teach the alternative: **sometimes the turn order has begun because the floor is enforcing a sentence.**
- Correction Writs may appear as environmental elements before any Correction Body arrives. A scene that opens with impossible notices and unsigned seals is a scene in which the Correction has already begun.
